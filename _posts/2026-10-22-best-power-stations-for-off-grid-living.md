The wrong one leaves you rationing watts and resetting breakers at dinner.

## What to look for

- **Usable capacity vs. nameplate capacity.** A "2,000Wh" station rarely delivers 2,000Wh at the outlet. Expect 85–90% usable after inverter losses, and plan for at least two days of your critical loads so a cloudy stretch doesn't strand you.
- **Continuous output and surge.** Continuous watts run your loads; surge watts start motors (fridges, pumps, tools). Off-grid living means inductive loads, so a high surge rating matters as much as the continuous number.
- **Solar input ceiling and MPPT quality.** A high max solar input (800W+) lets you recharge in a single good day. Built-in MPPT tracking and multiple input ports make a real difference when panels are partially shaded.
- **Expandability and cycle life.** Look for LFP (lithium iron phosphate) chemistry rated 3,000+ cycles, the ability to add battery modules, and pass-through/UPS behavior so your gear never reboots when you switch sources.

## FAQ

**How much power station capacity do I need for off-grid living?**
Start by adding up your daily critical loads in watt-hours — fridge, lights, Starlink, laptop, phone charging — then double it so you have a cloudy-day buffer. Most small-cabin and van setups land between 2kWh and 4kWh of usable capacity, while a full house with a well pump needs 6kWh or more.

**Can a power station really run a refrigerator off-grid?**
Yes, but check the surge rating, not just the continuous watts. A fridge compressor draws a brief surge on startup that can be two to three times its running watts, so a station with a high surge rating and at least 1,500W continuous will handle a standard fridge reliably.

**How many solar panels do I need to recharge a power station daily?**
Match your panel wattage to your daily consumption plus a margin — roughly 1.2 to 1.5 times your daily watt-hours in panel watts, assuming four to five peak sun hours. A 400–600W array is a realistic daily-charge setup for a 2kWh station in most of the US.

**Is LFP better than the older lithium-ion chemistry for off-grid use?**
For off-grid living, yes. LFP (lithium iron phosphate) is rated for thousands more cycles, is more thermally stable, and holds up better to daily charge-discharge cycling, which is exactly what an off-grid system does. The tradeoff is slightly more weight for the same capacity.

**Can I connect a power station to my home's electrical panel?**
Some units — the Anker SOLIX F3800 and EcoFlow Delta Pro Ultra among them — are designed to tie into a transfer switch or subpanel, but you need a licensed electrician to do it safely and legally. Smaller portable units are not meant for panel connection and should only feed appliances directly.
