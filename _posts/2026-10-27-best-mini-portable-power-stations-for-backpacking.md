## What to look for

- **Weight per watt-hour, not just weight.** A 300Wh unit sounds great until you realize it's 7 pounds. For backpacking, aim under 5 pounds total and look at the ratio — roughly 100–150Wh per pound is a good modern benchmark.
- **Output ports that match your gear.** USB-C PD at 60W or higher charges laptops and phones fast; a 12V car port or AC outlet (even a small one) matters if you're running a CPAP, camera charger, or drone. Pure sine wave AC output is non-negotiable for electronics.
- **Solar input compatibility.** If you're out for more than three days, you'll want to recharge from a folding panel. Check the panel's voltage against the station's input range — a mismatch means slow or no charging.
- **Real capacity vs. usable capacity.** Most stations lose 10–15% to inverter overhead. A "300Wh" unit delivers closer to 260Wh to your devices. Size up if you're powering anything with a heating element.

## FAQ

**How many watt-hours do I actually need for a three-day backpacking trip?**
Most backpackers use 30–60Wh per day for a phone, headlamp, and satellite messenger, so a 150–200Wh unit covers three days with margin. Add 50–80Wh per day if you're charging a camera or laptop, and size up accordingly.

**Can I charge a mini power station from a folding solar panel on trail?**
Yes, but check the panel's voltage against the station's input range first — most units accept 12–24V, and a 100W panel with the right connector will refill a 250Wh unit in about three hours of good sun. Panels under 60W are usually too slow to be practical on a multi-day trip.

**Will a mini power station run a CPAP machine?**
A 250–300Wh unit with a pure sine wave AC outlet will run most CPAP machines on low pressure for one to two nights, but check your machine's wattage — heated humidifiers draw far more and can drain a small unit in a single night. Turn off the humidifier to extend runtime.

**Is LiFePO4 worth the extra cost over older battery chemistry?**
For anything you'll use more than a few times a year, yes. LiFePO4 cells typically last three to five times as many charge cycles as NMC, so the higher upfront price usually pays off within a few years of regular use.

**Can I take a mini power station on a plane?**
No — power stations with capacities above 100Wh are generally prohibited in carry-on and checked baggage by TSA and FAA rules. For flights, carry a standard sub-100Wh battery bank instead, and ship or rent a power station at your destination.
