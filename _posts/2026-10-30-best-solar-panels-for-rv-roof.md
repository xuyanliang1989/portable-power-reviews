You're three days into a two-week boondocking trip and your house batteries are sagging to 11.9 volts by 9 p.m. Every night. The portable folding panel you've been dragging out and angling at the sun is fine for a weekend, but you're tired of chasing shade with a $200 suitcase every afternoon, and the generator noise is starting to feel like a moral defeat. Roof-mounted solar is the fix — but the RV roof is a genuinely hostile place for a panel. It flexes, it bakes at 160°F, it collects tree sap, and it's full of vents and skylights that steal your mounting space. Pick the wrong panel and you'll be resealing screws and chasing micro-cracks by year two. This guide covers the panels that actually hold up up there.

## What to look for

- **Cell type and efficiency.** Monocrystalline panels are the default for RVs now — higher output per square foot than poly, which matters when roof real estate is scarce. Efficiency in the 20–23% range is common on premium panels; anything much lower means a bigger footprint for the same watts.
- **Physical durability.** Look for anodized aluminum frames, tempered glass, and a junction box with proper IP-rated sealing. Some flexible panels skip the frame entirely and bond straight to the roof — great for curved roofs, but heat dissipation is worse and lifespan is typically shorter.
- **Voltage and compatibility.** A "12V nominal" panel actually puts out 18–22V open-circuit, which is fine for PWM controllers but you'll want an MPPT controller to get the most out of it. If you're building a series string, check the max system voltage and the panel's Voc temperature coefficient.
- **Warranty and mounting.** A 25-year power output warranty is the industry standard for rigid panels; flexible panels often carry 1–5 years. Also check whether the frame has pre-drilled mounting holes or you'll be drilling your own.

## FAQ

**How many watts of roof solar do I actually need for full-time RVing?**
Most full-timers land somewhere between 400W and 800W of roof solar, paired with 200–400Ah of lithium battery. The honest answer is that it depends on your daily consumption — run a load audit for a week before you buy panels, because adding capacity later is more expensive than sizing right the first time.

**Can I mix different brands or wattages of solar panels on one roof?**
You can, but you shouldn't wire mismatched panels in series — the lowest-performing panel drags down the whole string. If you must mix, wire them in parallel with separate charge controllers, or keep identical panels on each controller. In practice, buying all your panels at once from one model line saves a lot of headaches.

**Do I need an MPPT controller or is PWM fine?**
PWM is acceptable for a single small panel and a short cable run, but MPPT typically harvests 10–30% more power, especially in cool morning conditions and when panel voltage runs higher than battery voltage. For any roof array over about 200W, MPPT is the right call and the price difference is small.

**Will roof solar keep up with a residential fridge and an air conditioner?**
A residential fridge is realistic with 400W+ of solar and a decent lithium bank. Air conditioning is not — a typical RV AC draws 1,200–1,500W running, which would need a roof array several times larger than most RVs can physically fit. Plan on a generator or shore power for AC.

**How do I mount panels without leaking my roof?**
Use well-nut or plus-nut fasteners through the roof deck where possible, seal every penetration with a proper lap sealant (Dicor or similar) and re-check annually, and consider adhesive-mounted brackets or flexible panels if you're nervous about drilling. Never rely on screws alone into thin fiberglass without backing.
