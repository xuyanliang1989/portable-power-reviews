The wind has been howling for two hours and the lights just went out. Your phone is at 18 percent, the fridge is humming its last few minutes of cold, and somewhere down the hall your partner is asking whether the CPAP will make it through the night. This is the moment most people start shopping for backup power — not on a sunny Saturday, but at 11 p.m. with a flashlight in their teeth. A solar generator (a battery power station paired with folding solar panels) is the practical answer for a lot of households, but the category is crowded and the marketing is loud. This guide walks through the models that actually hold up when the grid goes down, what specs matter, and which one fits your situation.

## What to look for

- **Usable capacity vs. rated capacity.** A "1000Wh" station rarely delivers 1000Wh — inverter losses and depth-of-discharge limits eat 10–15 percent. Size for the appliances you truly need (fridge, medical device, router, lights) and add a buffer.
- **Continuous output and surge watts.** Continuous wattage runs your devices; surge wattage (usually 2x continuous for a few seconds) starts motors in fridges and pumps. Check both before buying.
- **Solar input limits and panel compatibility.** A high max solar input (400W+) lets you recharge fast on a clear day. Confirm the panel voltage window matches the station — mismatched panels are the most common buyer mistake.
- **Battery chemistry and cycle life.** LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) lasts far longer than older NMC lithium — commonly 3,000–4,000 cycles vs. 500–800 — and is safer at high temperatures. For an emergency unit you'll keep for a decade, LiFePO4 is worth the premium.
- **Pass-through/UPS mode and output types.** If you'll run a CPAP or router continuously, a sub-20ms UPS switchover and a pure sine wave inverter matter more than raw capacity.

## FAQ

**How many solar panels do I need to recharge a 2,000Wh station in one day?**
On a clear day with good sun angle, plan for roughly 400–600W of panels to refill a 2,000Wh station in four to six hours, accounting for real-world losses. Portable folding panels in the 200W range work but will take most of a day. Rigid panels mounted at the right angle are more efficient per dollar if you have a permanent spot.

**Can a solar generator run a CPAP machine all night?**
Yes — most CPAP machines draw 30–60W without a humidifier, so a 1,000Wh station covers a full night with plenty of margin. If you use a heated humidifier, budget for 100–150W and size up. Look for a pure sine wave inverter and a UPS mode so the switchover doesn't wake you.

**Is LiFePO4 worth the extra cost over regular lithium for an emergency unit?**
In my experience, yes. LiFePO4 typically delivers 3,000–4,000 cycles versus 500–800 for older NMC cells, and it's more thermally stable. Since an emergency unit often sits unused for months and then gets cycled hard during an outage, the longer-lived chemistry is the safer long-term buy.

**How long can a solar generator keep a refrigerator running?**
A typical full-size fridge draws about 1–2kWh per day in normal use, so a 2,000Wh station covers roughly one day before recharging. Pair it with 400–600W of solar and you can extend that indefinitely in sunny weather. Opening the fridge less often and keeping it full of water bottles helps stretch runtime.

**Do I need a transfer switch to use a solar generator during a blackout?**
Not for plug-in use — you can run extension cords to individual appliances safely. A transfer switch or home integration kit is only needed if you want to power hardwired circuits like furnace blowers or well pumps. For most people, corded use plus a few strategic appliances is simpler and cheaper.
