The first time a power station dies on you at the worst possible moment, it's usually not dramatic — it's just quiet. Maybe it's the second night of a boondocking trip and your fridge has been cycling all evening, and the battery percentage is dropping faster than the weather app promised. Or the grid goes down at 6 p.m., you haul out the old lithium-ion box, and it's already lost a chunk of capacity to a couple of years of sitting at 100% in a hot garage. That slow fade is exactly why so many buyers have moved to LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) chemistry. It lasts roughly 3,000–5,000 cycles versus a few hundred for older NMC cells, holds up better in heat, and doesn't degrade the same way when you leave it topped off. Here's what's actually worth buying right now.

## What to look for

- **Usable capacity vs. marketing capacity.** A "1,000Wh" label can mean 1,000Wh of usable energy or a nominal figure with a lower usable number after inverter losses. Check the rated output and whether the manufacturer quotes usable watt-hours.
- **Continuous output and surge.** Continuous watts run your devices; surge watts (usually 2x) handle motor and compressor starts. A 1,000W station that surges to 2,000W can start a small fridge but not a well pump.
- **Cycle life and warranty.** LiFePO4 should be rated 3,000+ cycles to 80%. Match that against the warranty — 5 years is now common, 10 years on premium units.
- **Charge speed and inputs.** Solar input limits, AC recharge time, and whether it supports pass-through charging matter more than raw capacity for van and RV use.

## FAQ

**How long does a LiFePO4 power station actually last?**
Most quality LFP stations are rated for 3,000–5,000 charge cycles to 80% capacity. At one full cycle a day that's roughly 8–13 years of daily use, though real life is usually gentler since most people don't cycle fully every day.

**Is LiFePO4 better than lithium-ion (NMC) for a power station?**
For most buyers, yes. LFP is more thermally stable, tolerates being stored at high charge better, and lasts far more cycles. The tradeoff is slightly lower energy density, so LFP stations are a bit heavier for the same capacity.

**Can I run a microwave or space heater off these?**
A 2,000W+ continuous station can run a typical microwave or a 1,500W space heater, but runtime is short — a 2kWh unit runs a 1,500W heater for roughly an hour before inverter losses. Check continuous watts, not just capacity.

**How much solar do I need to keep one charged?**
As a rule of thumb, plan for 200–400W of portable panels for a 1–2kWh station if you want a meaningful daily recharge in decent sun. Match the panel wattage to the station's maximum solar input so you don't waste capacity.

**Can I leave a LiFePO4 power station plugged in all the time?**
Most manufacturers say it's fine to leave it on AC for standby backup, since the BMS manages charge. For long-term storage, many recommend leaving it around 50–60% and topping it up every few months rather than storing at 100% in a hot space.
