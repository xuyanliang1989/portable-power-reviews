## What to look for

- **Usable capacity, not marketing capacity.** A 2,000Wh station that you only discharge to 20% gives you 1,600Wh of real power. Size your daily consumption first (fridge, laptop, heater, cooktop), then add 30% headroom for cloudy days.
- **Continuous output wattage and surge.** Van life means induction cooktops, kettles, and coffee makers — all high-draw resistive loads. You want at least 2,000W continuous if you plan to cook electric, and check the surge rating for compressor fridges and heaters.
- **Charging speed from multiple sources.** Look for simultaneous AC + solar + DC input. In a van, alternator charging (DC-DC) is often more reliable than roof solar, so confirm the station accepts a 12V/24V DC input at meaningful amperage.
- **Chemistry, cycles, and expansion.** LiFePO4 (LFP) is now standard and rated for 3,000–4,000+ cycles. Check whether the unit supports expansion batteries, has a UPS mode for shore power, and offers pass-through charging so you can run loads while topping up.

## FAQ

**How many watt-hours do I actually need for van life?**
Most van-lifers consume 1,500–3,000Wh per day depending on fridge size, cooking method, and electronics. Add up your daily loads, then size your station so you're only discharging to about 20% on a normal day, leaving headroom for cloudy stretches.

**Can a portable power station replace my van's house battery?**
It can, and many full-timers do exactly that — especially with high-capacity units like the Renogy Lycan 5000. The tradeoff is that portable stations typically charge more slowly from an alternator than a dedicated DC-DC charger and house bank, so plan your charging strategy carefully.

**Will these run an induction cooktop or microwave?**
Only units with at least 2,000W continuous output will reliably run a full-size induction cooktop or microwave. The Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus and Renogy Lycan 5000 have the highest continuous ratings here; the VTOMAN FlashSpeed 1500 will trip on those loads.

**How do I charge a power station while driving?**
Most units accept a 12V DC input, but the amperage matters — a station that only accepts 8A will charge slowly. Look for units that accept 12V/24V DC at higher amperage, or pair the station with a dedicated DC-DC charger for faster alternator charging.

**Is LiFePO4 worth it over older lithium-ion chemistry?**
Yes, for van life. LFP cells are rated for roughly 3,000–4,000 cycles versus 500–800 for older chemistries, they're more thermally stable, and they hold up better to daily deep discharge — which is exactly how a full-timer uses a power station.
