## What to look for

Before you compare models, lock in these four criteria — they matter far more than the sticker wattage.

- **Usable capacity (Wh), not "peak" capacity.** A 500Wh unit realistically gives you about 400–450Wh after inverter losses. Match that number to what you actually need to run.
- **Continuous output (W) and surge.** Continuous watts tell you what runs indefinitely; surge watts only cover motor startups for a second or two. A fridge might pull 150W running but 600W+ for a second on startup.
- **Chemistry and cycle life.** LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) batteries generally last far more charge cycles than older NMC lithium — often 3,000+ cycles versus 500–800. For a unit you'll keep for years, this is the single biggest durability factor.
- **Recharge speed and solar input.** How fast can it refill from a wall outlet, and what's the maximum solar input wattage? A unit that takes 12 hours to recharge is painful during a multi-day outage.

## FAQ

**Can a power station under $500 really run a refrigerator?**
Yes, but only the 1kWh-class units with at least 1,500W continuous output, like the Delta 2, AC180, or SOLIX C1000. A typical full-size fridge draws 100–200W running with a startup surge that can hit 600W or more, so you need both enough capacity and enough surge headroom. Smaller units in this price range will not run a fridge reliably.

**How long will a 1,000Wh power station run a CPAP machine?**
Most CPAP machines without a heated humidifier draw roughly 30–60W, so a 1,000Wh unit can typically run one for 12–20 hours depending on the exact machine and settings. If you use a heated humidifier, expect that runtime to drop significantly, and check whether your CPAP needs a DC adapter for efficiency.

**Is LiFePO4 worth it over regular lithium-ion?**
For a power station you plan to keep for years, yes. LiFePO4 batteries are generally rated for 3,000+ charge cycles versus roughly 500–800 for older NMC lithium, and they're more thermally stable. The tradeoff is usually slightly higher weight and cost, but in this price band most reputable units have moved to LiFePO4 already.

**Can I recharge these with solar panels, and how many watts do I need?**
Most units in this range accept 200–500W of solar input, and the Delta 2 and AC180 support up to 500W. In real-world conditions you'll usually see 60–75% of the panel's rated wattage, so a 400W array might deliver 250–300W. Match your panel's voltage to the station's input range before buying.

**What's the difference between continuous watts and surge watts?**
Continuous watts is what the station can output indefinitely, while surge (or peak) watts is a brief higher output that lasts only a second or two to cover motor startup. A fridge might run at 150W continuous but surge to 600W when the compressor kicks on, so you need a station whose surge rating clears that startup spike.
