The power goes out at 9 p.m. and suddenly you're doing math by flashlight: the CPAP needs to run all night, two phones are at 12%, and the router just died so the smart thermostat is useless. You don't need a 2,000Wh monster bolted to a hand truck — you need something you can carry with one hand that covers a night of essentials and a weekend of camping. That's the 300Wh sweet spot: light enough to toss in a trunk, big enough to run a laptop, a fan, a light, and a medical device through the dark hours. Here's what actually works in that class right now.

## What to look for

- **Usable capacity and chemistry:** Most "300Wh" units are 280–330Wh. LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) cells are rated for 3,000+ cycles versus roughly 500–800 for older NMC packs, so check which chemistry you're buying.
- **Output power, not just watt-hours:** A 300Wh battery with a 300W inverter can't start a 600W microwave. Look for at least 300W continuous AC output, and check the surge rating for anything with a motor.
- **Charging speed and inputs:** Wall recharge time varies from about 1 hour to 8 hours in this class. Solar input limits (often 60–100W) determine how fast you refill off-grid.
- **Ports and extras:** USB-C PD output at 60W or higher, a regulated 12V car port, and a built-in light matter more day to day than a fancy app.

## FAQ

**How long will a 300Wh power station run a CPAP?**
Most CPAP machines draw 30–60W without the humidifier, so a 300Wh unit should cover roughly 5–9 hours. With the heated humidifier on, draw can jump to 90W or more, which cuts runtime to about 3 hours — disable humidification for overnight outages.

**Can a 300Wh power station run a mini fridge?**
Only briefly. A typical mini fridge draws 40–60W running but needs a surge of several hundred watts to start the compressor, and it cycles on and off all day. A 300Wh unit will run one for a few hours at most; for all-day fridge duty you want 1,000Wh or more.

**Is LiFePO4 worth it over older lithium-ion in this size class?**
In my experience, yes. LiFePO4 packs are rated for thousands of cycles versus hundreds for older NMC cells, and they're more thermally stable. The tradeoff is slightly more weight per watt-hour, which is minor at this size.

**How many solar watts do I need to recharge a 300Wh station in a day?**
Plan on roughly 60–100W of panel input for a full recharge in one good sun day, since real-world panel output is usually 60–70% of the rated figure. Check your unit's maximum solar input before buying panels — exceeding it won't help and may be rejected by the controller.

**Can I take a 300Wh power station on a plane?**
Generally no. The FAA limits lithium batteries to 100Wh in carry-on without airline approval, and 300Wh units exceed that. These are road-trip, camping, and home-backup devices, not carry-on items.
