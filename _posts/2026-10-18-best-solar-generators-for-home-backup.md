The storm knocked the power out at 6:40 p.m., and by 7:15 the house was already going dark in stages — the fridge cycling off, the Wi-Fi dead, the sump pump silent in the basement. You've got a gas generator in the garage, but it's loud, it drinks fuel, and you can't run it on a balcony or in a garage without risking carbon monoxide. That's the moment a lot of homeowners start looking at solar generators: battery power stations you can charge from the sun and roll into the living room when the grid drops. The catch is that "solar generator" covers everything from a 300Wh camping box to a whole-home system that costs more than a used car. This guide sorts out which ones actually make sense for home backup, what the real prices look like in 2026, and where the honest limits are.

## What to look for

- **Usable capacity vs. headline capacity.** A 2,000Wh battery won't run your fridge for two days. Do the math on your critical loads (fridge ~1–2kWh/day, CPAP ~0.3kWh/night, sump pump bursts) before you buy.
- **Continuous output and surge.** A 1,500W unit won't start a well pump or a furnace blower that needs 3,000W+ to kick over. Check both continuous watts and surge watts.
- **Expandability.** For home backup, the ability to add battery modules later matters more than a slightly bigger built-in battery. Look for a unit with a documented expansion path.
- **Charging speed and inputs.** You want AC wall charging fast enough to top up between outages, plus real solar input (not a token 100W port) and ideally a transfer-switch or home-panel option.

## FAQ

**How many watt-hours do I actually need for a home outage?**
Start by adding up your critical loads. A fridge runs roughly 1–2kWh per day, a CPAP is about 0.3kWh per night, and lights and internet add a bit more. A 2kWh unit covers a day of essentials; for multi-day outages, look at expandable systems that let you add batteries.

**Can a solar generator run my furnace or central AC?**
Smaller units can't — a furnace blower or AC compressor needs a big surge. You need a unit with 240V output and high surge ratings, like the F3800 or Delta Pro Ultra, and often a transfer switch installed by an electrician.

**Do I need solar panels, or can I just charge from the wall?**
For short outages, wall charging between storms is usually enough. Solar matters for multi-day outages or off-grid use — check that the unit's solar input is substantial, not just a token 100W port.

**How long does it take to recharge from solar?**
It depends on the panel wattage and the battery size. A large battery bank with a big array can recharge in a few hours of good sun; a small unit with a folding panel may take most of a day. Check the max solar input spec before buying.

**Is a solar generator cheaper than a gas generator?**
Up front, usually not — a comparable-output gas generator costs less. But solar generators need no fuel, produce no exhaust, and run silently indoors, which matters for apartments and for overnight use. Factor in fuel and maintenance over several years and the gap narrows.
