The power goes out at 9 p.m. and suddenly the whole house is dark except for your phone flashlight. You've got a CPAP machine that needs to run all night, a fridge full of food you'd rather not throw out, and a router that's the only way to check the outage map. A 1000W portable power station is the sweet spot for this exact moment — big enough to run a full-size fridge, a microwave in short bursts, or a CPAP for a couple of nights, but still light enough that one person can haul it to the car. The problem is that "1000W" means very different things across brands, and the marketing on these boxes is genuinely confusing. Here's what actually matters, plus the units I'd put my own money on.

## What to look for

- **Real continuous output, not just peak.** A "1000W" unit should sustain 1000W continuous (running watts) and handle a higher surge for motor loads like fridges and compressors. Check the continuous number, not the big number on the box.
- **Battery capacity in Wh.** Output wattage tells you what it can run; watt-hours tell you for how long. Around 1000Wh is the typical pairing with a 1000W inverter — less than that and you'll drain it fast on heavy loads.
- **Recharge speed and inputs.** Look for AC recharge under ~2 hours, plus solar input (watts and voltage range) and ideally car charging. LiFePO4 batteries generally outlast older NMC chemistry in cycle life.
- **Outputs and pass-through.** Count your AC outlets, USB-C PD wattage, and whether it supports UPS-style pass-through so it can sit between the wall and your router or desktop.

## FAQ

**How long will a 1000W power station run a full-size fridge?**
A typical full-size fridge draws around 150–300W when the compressor is running but cycles on and off, averaging maybe 1–2 kWh per day. A 1000Wh unit will usually keep a fridge cold for roughly 12–24 hours depending on ambient temperature and how often you open the door. Pairing it with a solar panel extends that significantly.

**Can a 1000W power station run a microwave?**
Most 1000W-class units can run a small microwave (typically 700–1000W cooking power, which draws more from the wall) for short bursts, but you need to check the microwave's actual input wattage, not just its cooking wattage. Units with 1500–1800W output like the Delta 2 or AC180 handle microwaves more comfortably than true 1000W units.

**Is LiFePO4 worth it over older battery chemistry?**
In most cases, yes. LiFePO4 batteries generally offer a longer cycle life, better thermal stability, and more consistent performance over years of use than older NMC chemistry. The trade-off is usually a slightly higher price and a bit more weight, but for a unit you plan to keep for 5–10 years, it's the better long-term buy.

**Can I use a 1000W power station for a CPAP machine overnight?**
Yes — most CPAP machines draw well under 100W, and many draw far less without the humidifier. A 1000Wh unit will typically run a CPAP for two or more nights, and the clean sine-wave output on units like the Jackery or EcoFlow is safe for medical devices. Check your machine's actual draw and whether you use the heated humidifier, which roughly doubles consumption.

**How many solar panels do I need to recharge a 1000W power station?**
To recharge a ~1000Wh battery in a full sunny day, you generally want 200–400W of solar input, depending on the unit's maximum solar input rating and real-world panel efficiency. Check the station's solar input wattage and voltage range before buying panels, since exceeding the voltage limit can damage the unit. Folding 200W panels are a common pairing for camping.
