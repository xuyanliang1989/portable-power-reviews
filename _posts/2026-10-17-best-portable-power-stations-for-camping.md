This guide walks through the models worth your money in late 2026, what specs genuinely matter, and where each one fits.

## What to look for

- **Usable capacity vs. advertised capacity.** A "1,000Wh" label usually means ~85–90% is usable before the inverter cuts out. Match capacity to your real loads: a 12V fridge draws roughly 40–60Wh per hour cycling, a CPAP with humidifier can pull 300–500Wh overnight.
- **Continuous AC output and surge.** You need continuous watts to cover everything running at once, plus surge headroom for motors and compressors. A 500W station cannot start a 700W microwave no matter what the box says.
- **Charging speed and input options.** Solar input wattage (and whether it accepts parallel panels), AC recharge time, and car-port charging all matter when you're off-grid for days. Look for MPPT solar controllers.
- **Weight, ports, and chemistry.** LiFePO4 batteries last far more cycles than older NMC packs. Check the weight against how far you'll carry it, and confirm you have USB-C PD, 12V outputs, and enough AC outlets for your gear.

## FAQ

**How many watt-hours do I need to run a 12V camping fridge overnight?**
Most 12V fridges draw 40–60Wh per hour while the compressor cycles, so a full night typically uses 400–700Wh depending on ambient temperature and how often you open it. That means a 1,000Wh station is the realistic minimum for fridge duty plus device charging, and you'll want solar to refill it daily.

**Can a portable power station run a CPAP machine all night?**
Yes, but check the draw: a CPAP without a humidifier typically uses 30–60W, while a heated humidifier can push it to 90W or more. A 300Wh station can usually cover one night without the humidifier; for humidified use, step up to a 1,000Wh unit and confirm the station's continuous output exceeds the CPAP's peak draw.

**Do I need LiFePO4 or is a regular lithium battery fine?**
LiFePO4 is worth the premium for camping because it handles far more charge cycles (often 3,000–4,000 versus 500–800) and is more thermally stable. If you camp a few times a year, an older NMC pack is fine, but for regular off-grid use, LiFePO4 pays off over the life of the unit.

**How much solar panel wattage do I need to recharge a 1,000Wh station in a day?**
As a rule of thumb, plan for 200–400W of solar to refill a 1,000Wh station in a full sunny day, accounting for real-world panel efficiency and angle losses. Confirm the station's maximum solar input wattage before buying panels, since exceeding it won't charge faster and may be rejected.

**Can I take a portable power station on a plane or in a checked bag?**
No — power stations with capacities above 100Wh are generally prohibited in both carry-on and checked baggage by US airlines and the TSA. For air travel, stick to sub-100Wh power banks; leave the camping station in the vehicle or ship it separately.
