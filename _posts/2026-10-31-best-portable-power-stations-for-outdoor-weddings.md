Here's what actually works.

## What to look for

- **Usable capacity (Wh), not marketing watts.** A 1,000 Wh station runs a 60W string-light setup for many hours, but a 1,500W coffee urn for only minutes. Match capacity to your real loads, not the biggest number on the box.
- **Continuous output and surge watts.** Resistive loads (heaters, coffee urns) pull steady power; motors and compressors (fridges, blenders) spike on startup. Check the continuous rating and the surge rating separately.
- **Output types and count.** You want pure sine-wave AC outlets (clean power for audio gear), USB-C PD for phones and tablets, and ideally a 12V/car port. Count the outlets — a DJ, caterer, and lighting rig all compete for them.
- **Recharge speed and portability.** Outdoor weddings mean no wall outlet nearby. Solar input, fast AC recharge, and a weight you can actually lift onto a golf cart matter more than a spec sheet suggests.

## FAQ

**How much power does an outdoor wedding actually need?**
It depends almost entirely on whether you're running catering equipment. Lights, a small PA, and phone charging typically draw a few hundred watts total and can run for hours on a 1,000 Wh station. Once you add warming trays, coffee urns, or induction burners, you're into 1,500W+ continuous territory and need a much larger unit.

**Can a portable power station run a DJ setup?**
Yes, as long as you check the continuous output against the DJ's total draw (mixer, speakers, laptop, lights) and use a station with pure sine-wave output. Powered speakers can spike on startup, so leave headroom above the continuous rating rather than running right at the limit.

**Will a power station keep string lights on all evening?**
LED string lights are very efficient — a typical set draws well under 100W, so a mid-size station runs several strands for many hours. Incandescent strands draw far more, so swap to LED if you're powering a large lighting display from a battery.

**How do I recharge a power station during an outdoor event?**
Most units support AC recharge from any venue outlet (often in about an hour on fast-charging models) or solar input from a portable panel. If the venue has no power at all, plan on solar plus a fully charged unit before arrival, and size your capacity with margin.

**Is it worth buying a power station just for one wedding?**
Often yes, because rental options are limited and the unit stays useful for camping, tailgates, and home outages afterward. If you truly only need it once, a smaller budget unit like the Vtoman covers basic lighting and charging without a big outlay.
