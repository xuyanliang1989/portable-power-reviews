## What to look for

- **Usable capacity vs. advertised capacity.** A "1000Wh" station rarely delivers 1000Wh — inverter losses and depth-of-discharge eat 10-15%. Plan on roughly 85% of the number on the label.
- **Continuous output wattage.** This is what limits what you can actually run. A 1,000W station won't start a 1,500W microwave; a 2,000W+ unit with a surge rating will. Check both continuous and peak.
- **Battery chemistry and cycle life.** LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) lasts 3,000-4,000+ cycles and handles heat better than older NMC lithium. At this price point, LiFePO4 should be your default expectation in 2026.
- **Recharge speed and input options.** Solar input wattage, AC recharge time, and whether it can charge from a car or a wall simultaneously matter more than most spec-sheet features.

## FAQ

**Can a power station under $1000 run a full-size refrigerator?**
Yes, most 1,000Wh+ stations with a 1,500W or higher inverter will run a standard fridge for 12-24 hours depending on the fridge's efficiency and how often the door opens. The compressor's startup surge is the real test, so look for a station with a surge rating of at least 2,000W — the EcoFlow Delta 2, BLUETTI AC180, Jackery Explorer 1000 v2, and Goal Zero Yeti 1000 Core all clear that bar.

**How many solar panels do I need to recharge one of these in a day?**
For a 1,000Wh station, plan on 300-600W of solar input to fully recharge in a typical sunny day, accounting for real-world panel efficiency and angle losses. The Anker SOLIX C1000 accepts up to 600W, while the EcoFlow Delta 2 and BLUETTI AC180 top out at 500W — pair either with two 200W panels and you'll refill in a long afternoon.

**Is LiFePO4 really worth it over older lithium-ion at this price?**
In 2026, yes — LiFePO4 is standard at this price point and offers roughly 3,000-4,000 cycles versus 500-800 for older NMC cells, plus better thermal stability. The practical difference is that a LiFePO4 station should last 8-10 years of regular use before capacity drops noticeably, while an NMC unit may need replacing in half that time.

**Can I use one of these indoors during a power outage?**
Yes — unlike gas generators, lithium power stations produce no exhaust and are safe to run indoors. Just keep them out of direct sunlight and away from heat, and don't run them in a sealed closet where the fan exhaust can't dissipate. They're the reason more people are skipping gas generators for short outages entirely.

**What's the realistic runtime for a CPAP or medical device?**
A typical CPAP draws 30-60W, so a 1,000Wh station will run one for roughly 15-25 hours depending on the humidifier setting — enough for one to two nights. If you rely on a medical device, size up to a 1,500Wh+ station or keep a second unit charged, and check that your device's power brick works with the station's pure sine wave output (all the models here use pure sine wave).
