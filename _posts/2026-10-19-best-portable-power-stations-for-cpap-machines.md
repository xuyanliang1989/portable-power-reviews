It's 2:14 a.m., the power goes out, and your CPAP stops mid-breath. You wake up groggy, fumble for a flashlight, and spend the rest of the night wondering whether you'll get any real sleep before work. For anyone on CPAP or BiPAP therapy, a power station isn't a camping luxury — it's medical equipment insurance. The catch is that CPAP machines are picky: heated humidifiers and heated tubing can triple the draw, and a power station sized for a phone and a fan may not last until sunrise. This guide walks through the stations that actually work for sleep therapy, what specs matter, and how to size one honestly.

## What to look for

- **Usable capacity, not marketing capacity.** A CPAP without a humidifier typically draws 30–60 Wh per night; with the heated humidifier on, many machines pull 150–300 Wh or more. Multiply your machine's real draw by 8 hours and add 20–30% headroom.
- **True sine-wave AC output.** CPAP blowers and internal electronics prefer clean power. Pure sine wave is the safe choice; modified sine wave can cause hums or faults on some machines.
- **12V DC output (car port or barrel adapter).** Running your CPAP on DC skips the inverter losses and can stretch runtime by 20–30%. Check that an adapter exists for your specific model.
- **Pass-through charging and quiet operation.** You want to charge the station during the day and run the CPAP silently at night. Fan noise above ~30 dB can be noticeable in a bedroom.

## FAQ

**How many watt-hours does a CPAP use per night?**
Most CPAP machines draw roughly 30–60 Wh per night without the humidifier, and 150–300 Wh or more with the heated humidifier and heated tubing on. Check your machine's label or manual for its actual draw, then multiply by 8 hours and add 20–30% headroom.

**Can I run my CPAP on DC instead of AC?**
Yes, and it's often better. Many CPAP machines accept 12V DC through a manufacturer or third-party adapter, which skips the inverter losses and can extend runtime by 20–30%. Confirm an adapter exists for your exact model before buying.

**Will a power station wake me up with fan noise?**
Some will. Smaller stations with high inverter loads run their cooling fans audibly. Look for units marketed as quiet, and check owner reports for noise levels — the Anker SOLIX C1000 is a common pick for bedside use for this reason.

**How long will a 1,000 Wh station run my CPAP?**
With the humidifier off, most users get several nights. With the humidifier on, expect one to three nights depending on settings and room temperature. Cold rooms increase humidifier draw, so test your setup at home before relying on it during an outage.

**Do I need a pure sine-wave inverter for CPAP?**
It's strongly recommended. CPAP blowers and internal electronics run best on clean power, and modified sine-wave output can cause humming or faults on some machines. All the stations in this roundup use pure sine-wave inverters.
