## FAQ

**How much can a $300 power station actually run?**
Most stations in this price range have a 300W AC output, which is enough for a box fan, a mini fridge, a laptop, a CPAP machine, LED lights, and phone charging — but not for anything with a heating element or a large motor. A microwave, coffee maker, space heater, or full-size fridge will either trip the inverter or drain the battery in minutes. If you need to run those, you're looking at the 1,000W-and-up class, which starts well above $300.

**Is LiFePO4 worth paying more for?**
Yes, generally. LiFePO4 batteries typically last 2,000–3,000 charge cycles or more, compared to roughly 500–800 for older NMC cells, which means a LiFePO4 station can last many years longer before its capacity degrades noticeably. If you plan to use the station regularly — for camping, van life, or frequent outages — the extra cost usually pays for itself. If you'll only use it a few times a year, it matters less.

**Can I charge a power station with a solar panel under $300 total?**
It's possible but tight. A 100W folding solar panel typically runs $100–$180, so pairing one with a $180–$200 station gets you close to the $300 mark. Just check that the station's solar input voltage range matches the panel's output — most sub-$300 stations accept 100W–200W of solar input, but the connector types vary, so you may need an adapter cable.

**How long will a power station run a CPAP machine?**
A typical CPAP without a humidifier draws around 30–60W, so a 280Wh station like the Anker SOLIX C300 can run it for roughly 5–8 hours on a full charge. If you use a heated humidifier, power draw can jump to 100W or more, cutting runtime to two or three hours. If you rely on a CPAP nightly, look for a station with at least 500Wh, or plan to recharge mid-sleep from a second battery.

**Are these stations safe to leave plugged in all the time?**
Most modern LiFePO4 stations, including the EcoFlow River 3 and BLUETTI EB3A, are designed to handle pass-through charging and can stay plugged in indefinitely without damage — the battery management system prevents overcharging. That said, if you're using a station in UPS mode to back up a router or modem, it's fine to leave it connected permanently. Just avoid storing any lithium station in a hot car or direct sun for long periods, since heat is the main thing that shortens battery life.
